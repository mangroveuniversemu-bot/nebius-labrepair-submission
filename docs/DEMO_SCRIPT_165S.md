### Why a handover matters

An analysis can run successfully and still support the wrong conclusion. LabRepair gives reviewers a reproducible handover instead of another confident explanation. This prototype shows what was checked, what was rejected, and what has not run.

### Inspect an untrusted proposal

Here is a fixed, synthetic invoice fixture. Only ledger dot py is editable as a proposal; the cases and runner are protected. The reviewed literal response suggests removing duplicate invoices. It is not output from a live NVIDIA model, and we do not execute its candidate code.

### A plausible diff is not approval

Source, request and policy hashes bind this diff to one exact workspace. A fresh local replay reproduces the saved decision: blocked. We have not verified an isolated backend or an enforceable spending limit. A plausible patch is therefore a reviewable proposal, not a tested or approved repair.

### A refusal is a product result

Now the negative control tries to change the protected runner. It is rejected. A separate bundle forges an accepted verdict and recomputes its checksum. Independent replay still refuses it, and the failure page contains no successful result or download links. A checksum cannot grant authorization.

### Do not rewrite the original science

The scientific record stays separate. In the existing synthetic Maya case, the submitted S value is two point six one; registered replay is one point nine six two five. The original claim remains blocked. Fixing invoice code cannot validate a Bell claim, and this is not a hardware experiment.

### A replayable handover, with honest limits

The three downloads preserve the offline bundle, fresh replay and historical context. The planned NVIDIA on Nebius integration still requires verified model access, covered credits, a monetary cap and real isolation. Real inference and isolated execution have not run. Our current deliverable is evidence another reviewer can inspect and replay without trusting model prose.
